Entrepreneurship and Product Design - ED223
Course Format: This is a 3-credit course (1-0-4-3). It is 1 hour lecture and 4 hours practical (lab) every
week.
Course Content:
1) Entrepreneurship Fundamentals
2) Business Management Fundamentals
3) Product Design
To cover the essential elements of entrepreneurship in a manner that can sensitize students on the
prospects, opportunities, and challenges in entrepreneurship and the potential for value creation from
prospective ideas. In addition, there will be multiple modules of different functions of a company on
Finance, HR, IT, & Marketing from industry experts.  There will an extended module focusing on
‘Product Design’ which will encompass aspects of design thinking and affordable innovation.
In addition to classroom teaching of essential concepts and theories, there would be a project-based
learning approach. These would be practical exposure to the industry & incubation centres. Cases
would be used to narrate actual journey of entrepreneurs and their enterprises (both successes and
failures) having a mix of start-ups/corporates.

Reference Book(s):
1) Innovation and Entrepreneurship – Peter F. Drucker; Harper Perennial, 1986
2) Zero to One – Peter Thiel with Blake Masters; by Penguin Random House UK, 2014

Assessment Method:
•
Individual assignment: 30% (combination of cases and quizzes)
•
Team assignment:20%
•
End semester exam: 35%
•
Attendance: 15%
Course Outcomes: Students will become aware of the importance of “Entrepreneurial Capital” being
a vital contributor to the growth of a nation’s economy and well-being. Given that they are essentially
going to be trained as “Engineers”, the course will enable them to appreciate as how to make/leverage
technology and process driven innovations for solving problems and how to convert their ideas into
marketable solutions.
In addition, right from the Vision, Purpose to creation and leading of larger established corporates
would be discussed for students to be knowledgeable about the industry eco-system too.
The emphasis in this course is on project-based learning leading to identification of problems worthy
of solving and proposing techno-economically viable ideas that could potentially solve them.
By the end of this course, students are likely to be inspired to explore entrepreneurship as a viable
career goal.
