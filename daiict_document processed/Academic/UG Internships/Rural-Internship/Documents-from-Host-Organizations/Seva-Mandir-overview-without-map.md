## **SEVA MANDIR – Genesis** 

In the pre-independence period 1900-1947, Udaipur was witness to increasing awareness among educationist and liberal thinkers, of the particular backwardness and political stagnation of Rajasthan. This realisation provoked a movement towards change and inspired certain individuals, who had imbibed ideas of voluntarism, to seize the initiative. The Late Dr. Mohan Singh Mehta, founder of Seva Mandir, was one such individual - a social worker and an educationist. 

Dr. Mehta, not only played a very important role in founding Seva Mandir in 1968, but was also an inspiration for progressive change throughout Udaipur District. He was a true liberal influenced by the thoughts of Rabindranath Tagore, Mahatma Gandhi, Dr. -- Kunzru, Pandit Madan Mohan Malviya and by the tenets of the scout movement. In the pre-independence era he served as Education Minister and Prime Minister of Banswara in the State of Mewar, as an Ambassador of independent India, and as Vice Chancellor of Rajasthan University. He set up Vidya Bhavan at Udaipur, one of the pioneer co-educational schools, in 1931, with the philosophy of treating every child as a unique individual and helping him/her grow into a citizen with a sound moral character and a deep sense of social responsibility. 

## **Context** 

The work area of Seva Mandir encompasses 583 villages and 16 urban areas, out of which 423 are located in Udaipur District. The remaining 160 villages are located in the adjoining Rajsamand district, of which 67 villages are part of World Bank’s District Poverty Initiative Project (DPIP) begun in 2002.  Seva Mandir works with around 70,000 households, influencing the lives of approximately 360,000 persons. 

Seva Mandir works in the rural areas of Udaipur District, situated in the southern part of Rajasthan and geographically defined by the Aravali ranges. The district is characterized by rocky and hilly terrain, low rainfall (400600mm/year), and extreme temperature variations (4-48[0] C). Today, the forested and common lands, which constitute 72.73% of the total area, are excessively degraded. Of the total area under cultivation in the district (17.58%), only 13.6% of the cropped area is under some form of irrigation. 

The rural population consists primarily of tribal (68%) and other disadvantaged communities whose resources are severely limited. People are dependent upon subsistence agriculture, animal husbandry, and wage labour for their livelihoods. Udaipur’s literacy rate as of the 2001 National Census was 59.26%.  However, Seva Mandir’s independent field assessments belie the confidence in these high statistics and reveal that in spite of high enrollment figures, roughly 50% of children of school-going age are out of school. 

In health, the women and children of Rajasthan rank amongst the worse in the country, with extremely high levels of malnutrition, infant mortality rate (IMR), and maternal mortality rate (MMR). The IMR for South Rajasthan stands at 86.3% per 1000 live birth.[] MMR for Rajasthan stands at 670 cases per 100,000 live births.[][] The region is plagued by high incidence of tuberculosis (TB) and malaria, at 420 cases per 100,000 of rural population and 4,458 cases per 100,000 of rural population respectively. The rate of iron deficiency Anaemia amongst ever-married women in South Rajasthan is 58.3%, while the Child iron deficiency Anaemia in the age group 6-35 months in South Rajasthan is 76.1%.[][] Improving the region’s population in terms of health and literacy is complicated by the lack of infrastructure and availability of social services. 

Thus political processes leading to self-governance and institutions based on democratic and egalitarian values are not endemic to the region.  The tradition of hereditary leadership has continued, and institutions continue to be

## **MISSION** 

Seva Mandir’s mission is to make real the idea of society consisting of free and equal citizens who are able to come together and solve the problems that affect them in their particular contexts. The commitment is to work for a paradigm of development and governance that is democratic and polyarchic. Seva Mandir seeks to institutionalize the idea that development and governance is not only to be carried out by the State and its formal bodies like the legislature and the bureaucracy but should engage citizens and their associations separately and jointly with the State. The mission briefly to construct the conditions in which citizens of plural backgrounds and perspectives can come together and deliberate on how thy can work to benefit and empower the least advantaged in society. 

## **ORGANIZATION PROFILE** 

Seva Mandir, with its head office at Udaipur City, is currently working in 583 villages in the six blocks (sub-districts) of Jhadol, Girwa, Badgaon, Kherwara, and Kotra in Udaipur District and Kumbhalgarh in Rajsamond District. It works mainly in the areas of Natural Resources Development, Education, Health, Women and Child Development, and Institution Building. The organization serves the rural population through its staff of 282 full time workers and 737 Para workers. Full time workers are stationed in offices at the Head, Block, and Zone levels, while Para workers provide services and leadership directly to their village community. 

## **STRATEGY OF WORK** 

The long-term strategy of Seva Mandir focuses on creating the social, institutional, and property base for a democratic and participatory approach benefiting and empowering the poorest sections in the society. The specific objectives are: 

To create sustainable improvements in the livelihoods base; 

To enhance people’s capabilities for self-development (both at individual and community level); and To create and strengthen village institutions.

## **1. Livelihoods** 

There is widespread livelihood insecurity in Udaipur District as a result of the region’s highly degraded terrain and over exploitation of natural resources, a situation which is further aggravated by the fragmented social fabric of the village. Denied a stake in the management of their natural resources, the local communities lack the incentive for 

investing in the rejuvenation and sustainable management of the same.  Through its Livelihoods Program, Seva Mandir attempts to improve the productivity of these natural resources.  Areas of work include: Afforestation, soil stabilization and erosion prevention, implementation of lift irrigation systems,  protection and management of pasturelands, construction of Anicuts (small dams), and watershed management.  The organization is also involved in the government-supported Joint Forest Management Program, which encourages local communities to become involved in the protection and management of forests. It is hoped that these efforts, which consider land as the cornerstone of people’s livelihoods, will result in not only increased productivity but also enhanced social solidarity. 

## **Sadhna:** 

In addition to Seva Mandir’s natural resource-based income generation activities, Sadhna is a non-farm income augmentation program for women.  The program provides training in needlework and appliqué patchwork to village women who are then able to fabricate a variety of hand-stitched and appliqué products such as bed sheets, cushion covers, bags, files, and diaries. The handicrafts are sold under the ‘Sadhna’ label from a retail outlet located on the Seva Mandir premises at Udaipur. In 2001, Sadhna broke even and even had a surplus, which was, distributed among the artisans. Presently around 200 rural women are involved with the programme. The programme attempt to combine good design inputs and concerns for quality with Fair Trade Principles and the core concern for enhancing women’s own capabilities & skills. 

**Seva Mandir has done afforestation and pastureland development work on 10,306 hectares of land, including 1315 hectares on common pastures and 237 hectares through the Joint Forest Management Program in collaboration with the government.  15.4 million seedlings have been raised and planted. Watershed treatment work has been completed on approximately 4823 hectares of land.  35 small dams (Anicuts) and 27 lift irrigation systems have been constructed in Seva Mandir’s work area.  (Figures as of March ’03)** 

## **Capabilities** 

Seva Mandir attempts to enhance the capabilities of the rural poor by working for improved literacy levels, better health status, and sensitization against oppressive gender relations. 

Adult education was Seva Mandir’s first program in 1969. Three decades later, the organization caters to the educational needs of all ages and gender groups through diverse means. The focus however, is on children’s education. 154 Formal education centres are being run in villages. Additionally two residential learning camps are held every year, catering to around 300 children. For youth, Yuva Shiksha Kendras are run by Seva Mandir and taught by Anudeshaks (Instructors) who are trained from within the village community. 39 Village Libraries are being run to provide sustained support to adults in their endeavor to be literate and to use it in their daily lives. Seva Mandir works with women to build their capacities, increase their confidence levels, and facilitate their ability to articulate their concerns and participate in all development efforts. Program activities target gender sensitization, sexuality, and reproductive health through trainings and workshops for all community members.  Self Help Groups (SHGs) cultivate awareness, financial independence, and spur leadership development. The childcare program of Seva Mandir works with the objective of catering to the health and nutrition needs of children in rural and urban areas. Supplementary nutrition health care and pre-school activities are undertaken with village children in the age group one to six, at 81 Balwadis by Seva Mandir. A day care center is run in the urban areas, to cater to the children of working mothers. 

A 24-hour phone based help line is also run by Seva Mandir to attend to the calls of children in distress. This program is being run in collaboration with Ministry of Social Justice and Empowerment. 

The health program concentrates on basic health issues through the organization’s extensive network of villagebased Para workers. Seva Mandir trained Para workers function as Village Health Workers, Traditional Birth Attendants, and Home Remedy Workers.  Health education broadly focuses on sanitation, hygiene, and nutrition while specifically targeting maternal health, childcare and dissemination of birth control information. The programme is now seeking to develop a focus on problems of malnutrition, maternal and infant health, tuberculosis, malaria and HIV/AIDS/STDs, all of which are deeply interlinked to the acute poverty in this region. 

**Seva Mandir runs 158 Non Formal Education Centers, 81 Balwadis, and 2 hospitals.  175 Traditional  Birth  Attendants  and  91Health  Workers  serve  child  delivery,  health,  and nutritional services to the community. 327 women’s self help groups (SHG’s) have also been formed.  (Figures as of March. ’03)** 

## **Village Level Institutions** 

The Institution Building program of Seva Mandir aims at fostering robust people’s institutions that are effective in bringing about sustainable development in remote rural areas.  Seva Mandir’s quest to enable effective selfdevelopment and participatory self-governance at the village level resulted in the genesis of the **Gram Vikas Kosh** (GVK), or village development fund.  The fund is a common property resource created and controlled by villagers to provide them the means of realizing their development needs. The GVK allows the locus of authority to shift from outside the community to the community members themselves wherein people can crystallize their own agendas for development. The program focuses on the development of village institutions based on the principles of good governance through the creation of a village fund that is managed in a democratic and transparent way. Seva Mandir endeavors to create capacity at the community level for self-development through effective village institutions. This has been understood at three levels – the village, the organization, and the NGO sector and society at large. To facilitate this, the village, **People’s Management School** (PMS) was set up in 1991. At the village level, PMS provides support for setting up management systems and prepares training modules. PMS enables Seva Mandir to associate with the larger development dialogue by way of organizing and attending workshops, conferences, and training programs. Additionally, it undertakes research studies and process documentations to aid in monitoring and evaluating organizational interventions and to increase the understanding of processes and interaction at the village level.

## **Other Areas of Work** 

Seva Mandir accommodates 60-70 **volunteers** each year who visit the organization in an effort to better understand the various dimensions of sustainable rural development. Volunteers come both from within India and abroad and associate with Seva Mandir for varying lengths of time. They are provided space to contribute, learn, and conduct research studies on various issues of development. 

The **Kunzru Library** at Seva Mandir’s Head Office is a Public Library and was started in 1973.  It contains numerous books and audio-video material in Hindi and English on a wide range of subjects. A **Documentation Centre** houses studies and reports of Seva Mandir’s experience in the field and in the development sector at large. It also has a range of reference material on various issues and sectors of development in written and audiovisual format, many of which are available for sale at the Documentation Centre. 

Seva Mandir maintains a **Training Centre** at Kaya village (on the outskirts of Udaipur). It primarily functions as a facility for organizing training programs for staff and villagers. This Centre is also shared with other organizations that wish to carry out their training programs.
