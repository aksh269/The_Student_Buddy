SC463: Quantum Computation
Course content
DA-IICT, B.Tech,
Objective:
This course is an introduction to the emerging field of Quantum Computers. Students are
expected to know Quantum Mechanics but it is not mandatory. The objective of this course
is to introduce the students to a different kind of logical system, namely, the quantum logic,
based on the superposition of the states 0 and 1. The phenomenon of entangled states and
its use in controlled operations are introduced. With this basic tools from the physics of
Quantum Mechanics the course intends to prepare the students to understand some of the
important algorithms in quantum computation, quantum Information and Cryptography.
Prerequisite: Quantum Mechanics, Linear Algebra.
1. Introduction to Quantum Mechanics
(i) Postulates of Quantum Mechanics.
(ii) Measurement in Quantum Mechanics.
(iii) The bra-ket and density operator formalism.
(iv) Composite (Multi qubit) System.
2. Introduction to Computer Science.
(i) Turing Machine.
(ii) Circuit model of computation.
(iii) Measures of complexity.
(iv) Energy for computation.
(v) Reversible gates.
3. Quantum Circuits.
(i) Qubits, Quantum Gates.
(ii) Controlled Operations.
(iii) Unitarity of gates.
(iv) Relation to reversibility.
4. Quantum Algorithms.
(i) Quantum Fourier Transforms.
(ii) Order finding and factoring.
(iii) Quantum Search Algorithms.
(iv) Quantum Cryptography.
5. Quantum Noise

Outcome:
The students learn the idea of qubits which implements parallel processing in quantum
mechanics. The introduction of gates as Unitary operators acting on qubits help them to look
at quantum computers as intrinsically reversible machines. The student learns a completely
new algebra of logical system based on subspaces of a vector space as opposed to the boolean
algebra based on subsets of a set. Controlled gates provides a concrete application of the
complex phenomena of entanglement and measurement in quantum mechanics. The students
learn the Deutsch’s Algorithm, the search algorithm and algorithms based on Quantum
Fourier Transform.
This includes Shor’s probabilistic algorithm for factoring an integer.
The students learn the interesting application of generating a private key between parties
using quantum phenomenon, rather than transmitting a predetermined key, in Quantum
Cryptography. The course prepares the student to do further reading or get into research in
the exciting field of Quantum Computation and Quantum Information.
