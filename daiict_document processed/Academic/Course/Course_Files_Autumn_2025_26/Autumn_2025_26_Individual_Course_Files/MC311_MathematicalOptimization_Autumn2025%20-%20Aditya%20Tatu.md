MC311 Mathematical Optimization (3-1-0-4)
Autumn 2025
1. Instructor: Aditya Tatu
2. Course Category: Core for B.Tech (Mathematics & Computing)
3. Prerequisite Concepts: Calculus - Taylor’s series, Taylor’s remainder theorem, Con-
tinuity and Differentiability of functions of one and several variables, Gradient of a
function, Jacobian matrix, Hessian matrix of a function. Linear/Matrix Algebra - Rank
of a matrix, Column space & Nullspace, Eigenvalues and Eigenvectors, Matrix Diago-
nalization, Norms and Inner products in Rn.
4. Course Summary: A lot of problems across engineering and management can be mod-
eled as optimization problems. Optimization problems come in various forms - Con-
tinuous and Discrete or Combinatorial Optimization being two of the most popular
ones. This course deals exclusively with Continuous optimization, and aims to intro-
duce both, theory and algorithms in Continuous optimization. The course will intro-
duce students to basic terminology in optimization, theory - conditions for existence
of points of optima(maxima/minima), and algorithms to solve optimization problems.
We will deal with both, linear and non-linear continuous optimization problems, fo-
cusing more on the latter.
5. Course Content:
(a) Basic terminology in Optimization: Local optima, Global Optima, Equivalence of
minimization and maximization, Convex sets and functions.
(b) Set Constrained/Unconstrained optimization theory: First and Second order Nec-
essary/Sufficient conditions for optima.
(c) Unconstrained optimization algorithms: Gradient Methods, Newton’s method,
Conjugate direction methods.
(d) Constrained non-linear optimization theory: Feasibility set and surface, Active
and inactive conditions, Lagrange multiplier, Karush-Kuhn-Tucker(KKT) condi-
tion.
(e) Special cases of Constrained Optimization: Linear programming, Basic feasible
solutions, Simplex method.
(f) Duality: Lagrangian duality, Geometric interpretation, Duality gap, Saddle point
interpretation.
(g) (If time permits) Constrained Optimization algorithms: Barrier & Penalty methods
6. References:
(a) An Introduction to Optimization, Chong and Zak, Fourth ed., Wiley.
(b) Linear and Nonlinear Programming, Luenberger and Ye, Fifth ed., Springer.

7. Course Outcomes: The student will be able to
(a) use the first and second order conditions to compute optima for small uncon-
strained optimization problems,
(b) implement line search, gradient descent, and Newton’s method for unconstrained
optimization,
(c) use the KKT conditions to derive conditions that an optima or a constrained non-
linear optimization problem has to satisfy,
(d) appreciate importance of optimization in Data Science applications.
